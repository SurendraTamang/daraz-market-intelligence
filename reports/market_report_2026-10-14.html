1 · Data and how far to trust it

The crawler read every category in Daraz’s sitemap and followed 52 categories past page 1 for as long as their pages still showed sales: 132,797 distinct products collected, 60,235 of them with sales. The analyses below use the 121,598 page-1 listings only, so every niche is compared at the same depth; of those, 44% show a sold count and 39% have a rating. Prices and sold counts were spot-checked against the live site during the crawl’s quality gate.

  • Daraz’s “X sold” behaves like a rolling recent window, not a lifetime total: between daily snapshots 5k+ listings’ counts fell, and out-of-season summer items fell steadily in October. So the counts track recent demand, and a week-over-week change measures acceleration. Counts are rounded above 1,000 (“2.7K sold”).
  • Page 1 is a popularity-ranked sample (top 40 per category). Niche totals understate big categories and favour established products.
  • 97% of listings say “No Brand”, so brand is not used as evidence.
  • Niches need at least 8 products with sales to be ranked: 2,172 qualify.

2 · Demand is extremely concentrated in a few products

The top 1% of products account for 33.6% of units, the top 10% for 73% and the top 20% for 85% (Gini 0.81). Winning a niche means displacing its few hero products, not matching the average listing.

Lorenz curve of units sold across products with sales. The further the curve bows from the dotted diagonal, the more sales concentrate in few products.

3 · Most niches are concentrated, so the open ones are rare

Seller concentration is measured with the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index: the sum of squared seller shares of a niche’s units, 0 to 10,000. Bands follow the classic US DOJ/FTC thresholds of 1,500 and 2,500. The 2023 Merger Guidelines lowered them to 1,000 and 1,800, so under the newer reading even fewer niches count as open. With 40 or fewer listings per niche, HHI also runs high for small niches: treat a single niche’s figure as indicative.

13% of niches are unconcentrated, 21% moderately and 66% highly concentrated. 197 niches are both above-median in demand and unconcentrated: the pool worth entering.

4 · Cheap sells, but price alone does not predict demand

Units peak in Rs 500–1k (29% of all units). Across individual products the rank correlation between price and units sold is only -0.20 (Spearman): within a price band, product choice and listing quality matter far more than being cheapest. This is a cross-section, not a price elasticity: it cannot say what happens if you change your own price.

Bars: units sold per price band. Line: median units per product in the band (right axis).
